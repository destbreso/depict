# Characters

Every board is presented by a guide, and the guide is a character. Two ship with the skill; any drawing can become a third.

| Character | `guide.character` | Who it is |
|---|---|---|
| The marker | `"marker"` (the default) | A felt marker with big eyes, white gloves, black shoes and its cap in the board's accent: depict's own guide, at home on any board. A stiff barrel: it hops and lands with a firm squash, and it can tip its cap. |
| Stuart | `"stuart"` | A fuzzy pink brain with round glasses, rosy cheeks, a little w of a mouth, short salmon arms and legs and big black shoes. Soft: he squashes deep when he lands, bounces, and wobbles on like a jelly; he pushes his glasses up before he explains. Created by Niki, age 7, who drew him in three pictures. A boy: he, him, his, and he wears what a boy would (the shared costumes without the hair bow). His own costumes, from her drawings, worn as two outfits: winter, `elf-hat` (green, with a white pompom and a striped band) and `mittens` (green); and Halloween, `spider` (sitting on his head). |

`guide: { character: "stuart", wear: ["elf-hat", "mittens"] }` puts Stuart on a board, dressed. Each character's presentation, its model sheet and three boards it presents, is in `examples/characters/<id>/` (`npm run characters` remakes them). A character keeps its own list of what it can wear, its skins and its ways of arriving and leaving; a script that asks for something its character does not have is refused with the list it does have.

## What a character is

A character is three things, and each one is written down in its module.

1. **Its look.** A body; a face whose eyes are components of their own (so they blink), with pupils that follow the pen and brows and a mouth for each feeling; rubber-hose arms from its shoulders and hands in three shapes (open, a fist, pointing); legs, shoes and a shadow on the floor. It is drawn by the board's own pen, stroke by stroke, the first time it appears.
2. **Its body: what it is made of, so how it moves.** The principles of character animation (squash and stretch, follow-through, secondary action: [Thomas and Johnston, The Illusion of Life, 1981](https://archive.org/details/illusionoflifedi00thom)) are applied in proportion to its material. A stiff body squashes a little and is still at once. A soft one squashes deep, stretches tall in the air, bounces, and its top keeps swaying over its feet after it lands or sets off, like a jelly on a plate.
3. **Its personality: what it does on its own.** How it likes to arrive and leave (the seed picks among its own when a board does not say), its quirks (Stuart pushes his glasses up in a quiet moment before he explains), and a line of traits that says who it is.

## Make a character from a drawing

This is a tool of the skill. Use it when someone gives you a drawing of a character (a photo of a child's drawing, a scan, a sketch: one picture, or several pictures of the same character) and wants it as the guide of their boards. They might say "turn my daughter's drawing into the guide", "make this a character for the slides", "use this drawing as the presenter", or `/depict make a character from these drawings: niki-1.jpg niki-2.jpg`.

**What to ask for, if it is not given:** the drawing or drawings (image files, or images in the conversation); a name for it; who drew it and whether to credit them; and anything the pictures do not say: which things are part of it and which are costumes, what it is made of (soft, stiff, bouncy, floaty) and what it is like. One picture is enough; several pictures of the same character tell you what stays (the character) and what changes (its costumes, expressions and poses).

**Then:**

1. **Study the drawings side by side.** Write down what is the same in every picture: that is the character. Measure its proportions against its whole height: how much of it is the body, how long its legs and arms are, how big its eyes or glasses, how wide it is. Note its colors and its signature details (for Stuart: the fuzzy outline, round glasses far apart on a long bridge, big pupils with glints, rosy cheeks, the little w of a mouth). What changes between pictures is a costume (a hat, mittens, a spider on his head), an expression (an open mouth) or a pose (arms out); the costumes of one picture, worn together, are an outfit.
2. **Decide what it is made of and who it is,** from the drawing and from what the person says. A brain or a jelly is soft; a robot or a box is stiff; a ball is bouncy; a cloud or a ghost floats. Its face and its look suggest its personality (glasses: curious and a little nerdy). Choose its intros and outros to match: a soft body bounces and jiggles; a stiff one hops, bows and salutes.
3. **Write its module** as the shipped ones are written (`kit/characters/stuart.mjs` is the worked example from a drawing; `kit/characters/marker.mjs` is the default). Draw it in its own frame: `u` across and `v` down from the top of its head, its feet at `v = 0.99`, both in units of its height, so its proportions are the drawing's own. Give its body, face and limbs as the drawing has them, its poses for its own body (short arms reach less), its places for the shared costumes, its own costumes from the drawings (and its outfits, one per variant picture), its material and its personality. The fields are listed below.
4. **Respect the original; improve only what a guide needs.** Keep its proportions, its colors, its signature details and its spirit, exactly. Improve the line (even weight, closed shapes, symmetry where the drawing was unsure), give it every feeling a guide shows (if the drawing has no brows, add small ones only for the feelings that need them: worried, surprised, sad, quizzical; the happy face stays as drawn), make its hands read at a small size, and keep its proportions the same in every pose. Never add what it does not have, and never "correct" it toward a style of your own.
   - **A child's animal or creature is drawn to be loved.** A child drew a friend, and a guide a child will love has appeal: a big round head on a small, plump body (the head about half its height), big eyes set low and wide on the face, each a colored iris with a dark pupil and two shines (a ring with a dot in it stares), the nose and mouth small and low, soft rounded shapes and short limbs, cheeks where a face has room for them, and an outline of even, moderate weight (a heavy black line reads hard). Keep every signature detail of the drawing (its colors, its ears, its whiskers, its tail, the way it said its paws or its mouth look) inside that shape. A zigzag mouth reads as fangs and a box of a head as a robot: draw a w as two soft bumps and a head as a rounded shape. When a drawing shows the creature from the side (a cat walking) and the guide must face the viewer, stand it up as a cartoon animal does, and say why in its module.
   - **Dress it only in what fits its body.** A costume is drawn only on a character whose body has a place for it: a hat needs a head to sit on, a bow tie or a scarf a neck, a star a chest, a bow hair, a pencil an ear. A character without one (a shoe has no head, a ball no neck) wears none of those, whatever slot it is drawn to: `wear` lists only the shared costumes that fit it (none when it is left out), each where it sits on its own body (`anchors`), and `scripts/character.mjs` refuses one listed without its place. Give it costumes of its own that suit what it is instead (a ribbon on a shoe's strap, a competition number on its side), and make its outfits for the occasions from those. Never put a costume on a body it does not fit to fill an outfit or a row of the sheet.
   - **No seams.** Every outline of a part lies over every fill of it, so two shapes of one part that overlap show a line through the one in front: give a shape the shapes in front of it (`shape(part, uv, color, { behind: [uv, ...] })`) and its outline is drawn only along the silhouette. Draw a small circle the pen must close (an eye) with `ink.ellipse(..., { closed: true })`: the pen's drift, which keeps a big circle from looking traced, turns a small one into a spiral, bigger or smaller at random, so two eyes would come out different.
5. **Check it before any board uses it** (and keep it to revisit later, Revisiting a character): `node scripts/character.mjs path/to/character.mjs --out <folder>`. It refuses a module with a missing or wrong field (every problem at once), then writes its model sheet (`<id>-sheet.png`: every feeling, every pose, the costumes it really has) and three test boards it presents (`<id>-test-1.html` to `-3.html`, each in other clothes, arriving and leaving its own ways). Put the sheet beside the drawings and compare part by part; play the tests and watch it land, point, step aside, hop and feel. Then show both to the person, and to whoever drew it, and change what they ask for. Only then put it on their boards.
6. **Use it.** A board can bring its own character: `import mine from "./characters/mine.mjs"` and `guide: { character: mine }`. To ship it with the skill instead, put its module in `kit/characters/` and add it to `kit/characters/index.mjs`; then `guide: { character: "<id>" }` works on any board.
7. **Credit the artist** when they want it: a `credit: { by, age, note }` field, and a line at the top of its module. The sheet's title carries the note.

## Revisiting a character

A character is revisited over time: its line gets cleaner, its motion smoother and more natural, a gesture clearer, a new way to move is added. What it is does not change in a revisit: its proportions, its colors, its signature details, its personality and everything its artist said about it. Each revisit improves it without losing its look:

1. **Keep how it is before changing it:** `node scripts/revisit.mjs <character> --out <folder>` writes its model sheet and test boards as they are now, a contact sheet of each test board and of the guide close up (a frame every two seconds), and a fingerprint of the sheet and of every frame (`revisit.json`).
2. **Change one thing at a time** in its module, and say in its top comment what changed and why.
3. **Hold it against how it was:** `node scripts/revisit.mjs <character> --out <new folder> --against <folder>` says what changed (the sheet, and which frames of which test board, from when to when) and puts the sheet and each test's frames side by side, then and now (`<id>-then-now.png`). Only what was meant to change may have changed. Put the new sheet beside the original drawings, part by part, as when it was first made: it must still be the drawing, only better drawn.
4. **Show a change to its look** to whoever drew it (or their family) before any board or page uses it, as the first version was shown.
5. **A change to the kit is a revisit of every character:** their sheets, looks and test boards, the example boards and looks cast into a board are compared before and after, and nothing may change that the change did not mean to change.
6. **Never correct it toward a style of your own**, and never smooth away what its artist made its own (a tilted face, a crooked smile, uneven eyes) unless they ask for it.

## Characters that share a slot

Every board keeps a slot for its guide: how tall it stands, the paper it takes beside each idea, the room over its head for what it wears, and how far its pointing finger reaches. The board is laid out by the slot, and the guide's performance is worked out with it (where it stands, how it hops, what it points at, when it steps aside), so a board is the same board whoever presents it. A character keeps its own slot unless it is drawn to another's: `contract: "stuart"` draws a character to Stuart's slot. Characters of one slot are interchangeable: any of them can present any board made for that slot, cast in as the board plays, with no board built again.

**A character drawn to another's slot** stands exactly as tall (`size` is the slot's, and its module may not say another). Its body is its own: round like Stuart, narrow as a pencil, or any other shape, with its own shoulders, arms, reach and poses. What it does not say it takes from the slot's character (its measures, shoulders, reach, poses, what a hat tips about, its arms and its hands), and poses it gives join the ones it takes, so it can add a quirk's pose without saying the rest again. It never takes the places for costumes: a costume fits a body, so a character says where each shared costume it wears sits on its own (`anchors`), even where that is where it sits on the slot's character, and wears none it does not say. `scripts/character.mjs` and `scripts/look.mjs` check that it fits the slot:

- at rest, its drawing and its hands stay within the slot's paper on either side, and it is no taller than the slot;
- a costume reaches no higher on it than on the slot's character, and its own costumes no higher than his highest;
- its hands are no more than a quarter bigger than his.

How far it points beside the slot's own character is reported, not required (`reachShare` in kit/look.mjs): a narrow character with short arms reaches less far (Niki's pencil, 68% as far as Stuart). Where a board has its guide point further than this character's finger reaches, or along a line its own arm would not keep clear, it looks at the thing instead, its hands at rest. So it never points across the drawing or at the wrong thing, whatever its shape.

**One file per character, for every board of its slot.** `node scripts/look.mjs <character> --out <id>.look.json` writes the character's look: its body and face, its arms and hands, each costume it can wear as a part of its own, its outfits, its ways to arrive and leave and its quirks, and what it is made of; 30 to 50 kB. A look does not depend on any board: a board built once and a look made once meet in the page. With `--out <id>.look.js` it is a script that puts the look in `window.depictLooks[<id>]`, for a page opened from the disk, where nothing can be fetched.

**Alive to a touch.** On any board, a tap on the guide has it do something of its own, a different thing each time: its quirks (its forms, its fidgets, a pose of its own) take turns with what every guide does its own way (a hop and a wave, and the jump, spin or jiggle among its ways to arrive, and a cheer). So what a child finds in a character by tapping it is what its personality says: give a character its quirks and its intros, and its taps follow. A long press or a right click on it is the host page's (`guide-menu`).

**Casting it in.** A page casts a look in its guide's place with `depict.cast(look, dress)`, or a host with a message to the page, `{ depict: "cast", look, outfit }`; `depict.cast(null)` (a message with `look: null`) brings the board's own guide back. The page answers a host with `{ depict: { event: "cast", ok, character, outfit, wear } }`, or with `ok: false` and the `reason` (a look of another slot, or a board built before slots). From that moment the new character does what the board's guide does, its own way:

- it goes where the board's guide goes, points where it points (or looks, as above), feels what it feels, and holds what it holds;
- it arrives and says goodbye its own way: the board's own way when that is one of its own too, otherwise one of its own picked by the board's seed, and an arrival never leaves the ground higher than the board's own did (the opening shot made room for that one);
- in the board's quiet moments, where Stuart pushes his glasses up, it does its own quirks, or rests when it has none;
- it moves as its own material does: a cloud settles slowly, a pencil lands firm;
- it wears its own outfit of the board's (below), or else the costumes the board wears that it can wear, and a costume goes on only where the board kept the room over its head. A board made to be dressed as it plays (a page where a child picks the outfit) keeps room for every costume: `guide: { room: "all" }`.

**Outfits: the occasion and the part.** An outfit is costumes worn together under a name. The name is what a board and a host ask for, so each character wears its own:

- **Its own outfits, from its drawings**, like Stuart's `winter` (an elf hat and mittens) and `Halloween` (his spider). A board dresses its guide in one by name (`guide: { outfit: "winter" }`), and a board whose list of things is one of its guide's outfits is dressed in that outfit.
- **The parts every character can dress for**, from the shared costumes that fit its body, unless it has its own outfit of that name (a shoe has no head for a hard hat, so it dresses as a builder only in a builder's outfit of its own): `builder` (a hard hat), `scholar` (a mortarboard), `chef`, `party`, `royal` (a crown), `music` (headphones) and `formal` (a bow tie). A board about building dresses its guide as a builder, and a friend with a builder's outfit of its own (a tool belt it was drawn with) wears that one.
- **The occasions of the year.** A host that asks for `outfit: "today"` dresses a character in its own outfit for the day, when it has one: New Year, Valentine's Day, St. Patrick's Day, the Fourth of July, Halloween, Thanksgiving and Christmas, then the seasons, winter and summer (a holiday before its season; `OCCASIONS` in kit/act.mjs). A host can name any occasion of its own (a school's sports day), and a character with an outfit of that name wears it.

Give a character the outfits its drawings show: whoever drew it can draw it for an occasion too.

**The example** is `examples/friends/`: the hash table board built once and presented by Stuart as a scholar (`friends.html`); the looks of Stuart, of Pip (a rain cloud drawn to his slot with his body and its own face, colors, material, outfits and quirk) and of Niki's pencil (narrow, with its own short arms); and a host page with a picker for who presents it and what for (`index.html`), as a site would have one. `npm run looks` makes the looks again.

## A body that moves its own way

What a character is (a cat, a cloud, a dog) often says how it moves beyond its arms and face, and whoever drew it may say so too (it rubs against your legs to say hello, it waves its tail as it walks away, it wiggles its ears while it thinks). Give it those, from what the drawing and its artist say, never from a type:

- **Parts that move on their own** (`swing`): a part drawn under a name of its own (`"tail"`, `"ear0"`) that turns about a pivot, behind the body: a tail sways all the time and, with `sides`, swings over to its other side every few seconds, passing behind the body; a part with `while` (ears that twitch) is still until its fidget comes. Every part that swings swings harder while the guide wags (its hello and goodbye, below).
- **Forms** (`forms`): whole-body drawings it takes in place of standing (a cat sits, curls up into a ball), drawn in `draw(k)` as parts `form:<name>`, in the order they lie (each over the ones before it). A form is taken in a quiet moment as a quirk, held while the moment stays quiet (up to `FORM_TIME`, 2.8 s, and not at all in a moment shorter than 1.6 s), or for a whole beat that asks for it (`act: "sit"`). Its arms, costumes and held things are not drawn while it is taken.
- **Fidgets** (`fidgets`): a quirk that moves parts rather than hands (its ears twitch, the parts whose `swing` says `while: "ears"`), with `look: "around"` for its eyes to look left, right and down meanwhile; in a quiet moment, or for a beat that asks for it (`act: "ears"`).
- **A pose held a while** (`hold`, seconds, on a pose of its own that is a quirk): held for that long while the moment stays quiet, and no longer, as a shoe balances on its tip while it thinks or waits (a pose whose body rocks about its tip: `{ hold: 2.4, body: { pivot, sway } }`). A pose without a hold is the quiet moment's second, as before; a tap holds it as long too, easing into it and out of it.
- **A shy character** (`personality.shy`, a pose of its own, like a glance away out of the corner of its eye): once it has arrived it takes that pose a moment (`SHY_TIME`) before it presents, and its first quiet moment is that pose too; then its quirks take turns, and a shy pose among them keeps a little of its shyness in small moments later on. It is shy the same way cast into another character's board, and the board's own guide's shyness goes with it.
- **A beat can ask for a pose of its own** (`act: "balance"`), as it asks for a form or a fidget: taken as the pen begins and held until its next gesture.
- **Its own ways to travel** (`personality.travel`: `hop`, `flip`, `roll`, `slide`, `tiptoe`): each trip from one idea to the next takes them in turn, from the board's seed, so a gymnast flips to one idea, rolls to the next and slides to a third. A flip turns it over once in the air about its middle each hop; a roll turns it over along the ground, rising only as far as its body needs (`motion.roll`, a share of its height); a slide keeps it on the floor, leaning back into it; on tiptoe it takes many quick little steps, in its `tiptoe` pose when it has one (a rise onto its toes). A step around the drawing takes only the ways that leave the ground (a hop, a flip), so it still passes over what is drawn. Where it turns over is its middle (`motion.middle`, or a view's own). Left out, it hops, as before.
- **Its own ways to arrive and leave**, among the shared ones: `rub` (it rubs against you, swaying side to side with its eyes shut in pleasure, its tail wagging, then waves) and `stroll` (it waves its tail, turns away and takes a few soft steps, still inside the closing circle); and a gymnast's `flip` (it springs up, turns over once in the air and lands in a pose; to leave, it turns away and flips off a little way) and `roll` (it turns over along the ground; to leave, it rolls away). A tap on it flips or rolls it too, when those are its ways.

A character cast in a board's place does its own forms and fidgets in that board's quiet moments, as it does its own quirks; a board made for another guide needs no building again for it.

## Another view of it

A character is drawn standing one way, its main view; it may have others it is seen in for as long as it needs, not only for a moment as a form: a shoe at rest on its sole, seen from the side, that faces the viewer to say hello; a car seen from the side that turns to the front to cheer. Name each other view in `views` (`{ front: { hat: [du, dv], wear: [du, dv], middle } }`: where its costumes move to in it, in units of its height, and the height of its middle from the top, for what turns or bends about its middle) and draw it in `draw(k)` inside `k.view("front", () => { ... return { eyes } })`: everything drawn there is that view's, its body and its whole face (its eyes as components of their own, which blink and look, its brows and mouths for the feelings, its squints), each part named as the main view's are. A pose says the view it is taken in (`poses.present.view: "front"`), and the guide is seen in that view for as long as the pose lasts, its face alive, its costumes moved with it; between two views it turns, narrowing to its edge and back, the drawing changing at its edge (`VIEW_TURN` in kit/act.mjs). Every view fits the slot it is drawn to, as its main view does; a view is not drawn by the pen as the guide first appears, but complete the first time it is taken. A character with arms keeps them where its main view has them.

**A solid turns by rotating.** A flat drawing turns by narrowing to its edge and back, and faces the other way as its mirror at once; a character drawn from a modelled shape (a shoe built as a real shape and seen through a camera in perspective) never goes flat. Say the angle each of its drawings is seen from, in degrees round its upright axis (its side 0, facing the viewer 90): its standing view's (`yaw`) and each other view's (`views.<name>.yaw`), and draw the angles a turn passes through as views of their own that no pose takes (`views.<name>: { yaw, between: true }`, drawn in `k.view` as any view is). Then every turn, into another view or to face the other way, rotates it over `VIEW_TURN`: at each moment it is seen in the drawing of the nearest angle it has, each facing either way (a drawing facing left is its mirror), so it passes its front as it turns round and its volume shows all the way; its eyes keep looking where it looks and a flip goes over the same way while the drawing it is seen in is mirrored. Its sheet has a row of its drawings from facing right to facing left. It has drawings only round its front, so it never spins: a spin is not among its ways to arrive or leave, and a tap does not spin it.

## A character with no arms

Some characters have no arms (a shoe, a ball, a drop of water), and they still present a board: they show things with their eyes and their whole body. Say `arm: false`, and leave out `hands` and the hand poses; never draw arms it does not have to give it something to point with. Each pose is then its body's (`poses.<name>.body`), the kit's own where the character says none (`GESTURES` in kit/act.mjs):

- **It shows a thing** (`point`, `present`) by bending toward it from its middle and looking at it (`aim`, in degrees), stretching up a little to a thing above it and sinking to one below. A board needs nothing else: where another guide would point, it leans in and looks.
- **It waves** by rocking (`sway: [degrees, seconds a rock]`), **cheers** by stretching up and bobbing (`pulse: [share of its height, seconds a bob]`), **shrugs** by sinking a little, **thinks** leaning back, and **nods** for a salute or a bow.
- **A pose of its own** (a quirk, a tap's reaction) says `lean` and `bend` (degrees toward where it faces), `stretch` (a share of its height, less than 0 to sink) and `look` (where its pupils go, `[x, y]` from -1 to 1): a shy glance to the side is `{ body: { bend: -4, look: [-1, 0.2] } }`.

**It can rise onto a point of itself**, as a dancer goes up en pointe or a shoe onto the tip of its toe: a pose whose body says `pivot` (the `[u, v]` it turns about, on its outline), `turn` (degrees, clockwise as it faces right: a shoe facing right with its toe tip as the pivot lifts its heel) and `shift` (`[du, dv]`, where it moves meanwhile, so it rises over its own spot and not off it), with `ease` (the seconds it takes to go up, and to come down again). A `sway` on a pose with a pivot rocks it about that point, as it balances there. Any character can rise, with arms or without; keep the risen figure inside the slot's paper, as its rest is.

It holds nothing (a board that gives it a pointer or a book is refused, and a look of it cast where another guide held one leaves the thing out), its tap reactions are its body's, and the hands of the slot it is drawn to are not measured. Any pose of a character with arms may also say a `body`, which moves the whole figure as its hands go.

## The module, field by field

A character is a module whose default export holds these fields. `scripts/character.mjs` checks every one.

| Field | What it says |
|---|---|
| `contract` | Optional: the slot it is drawn to, another character's id (`"stuart"`), so it can present any board made for that slot; leave it out for a slot of its own. Its `size` is then the slot's, and what it does not say it takes from that character (see Characters that share a slot). |
| `id`, `name` | A short lowercase id (`"stuart"`) and how a sentence names it (`"Stuart"`, or `"the marker"`). |
| `about`, `credit` | One line on who it is; who drew it (`{ by, age, note }`), if they want the credit. |
| `size` | Optional: its height beside the marker's (1). A wide character stands a little shorter, so the shots keep their size. |
| `measures` | The paper it takes, in units of its height, from the point between its feet: `back` and `front` at rest (arms and hands included), and `reach`, to the tip of a pointing finger. The kit keeps that paper free for it beside each idea. |
| `shoulders` | `{ front, back }` as `[u, v]`: where its arms come out of its body. |
| `arm` | `{ width }` in units of the outline's nib; a limb with a color of its own adds `fill` (a color) and `fillWidth` (its arms are then an ink outline round that color). `false` for a character with no arms (A character with no arms). |
| `reach`, `tip` | How far its arm reaches from its shoulder, and how long its pointing finger is, in units of its height (the marker: 0.36 and 0.13; Stuart's short arms: 0.21 and 0.1). |
| `capPivot` | `[u, v]`: what a hat tips about (the marker's cap rim; the top of Stuart's head). |
| `poses` | For a character with no arms, what its body does in each (`body`: lean, bend, stretch, look, aim, sway, pulse, and a rise: pivot, turn, shift, ease), the kit's own where it says none; any pose may also say the `view` it is taken in. Otherwise, where its hands go, for its own body: `rest`, `present`, `wave`, `cheer`, `shrug`, `scratch`, `salute`, `tip` and `point`, each `{ front, back }` with `hand` and `bow` (the hose's curve) as `[u, v]` and a hand shape (`open`, `fist`, `point`); and any pose its quirks need (Stuart's `glasses`). |
| `hands(wear, colors)` | Not for a character with no arms. Its hands for what it wears: `{ size, shapes: { open, fist, point }, cuffs, knuckle, fill }`, the shapes as outlines in units of the hand's size, `fill` a color or `"paper"` (white gloves). Stuart's become green mittens when he wears `mittens`. |
| `draw(k)` | Its body, face, legs and shadow, drawn with the kit's pen in the order a hand would draw them; it returns `{ eyes }`, the centers of its eyes. `k` gives the pen (`p`), its height (`h`), `P(u, v)` (its frame to the board's), the outline's nib `W` and a `fine` one, `add(part, role, lines, width, color, layer, opacity)`, `shape`, `line`, `disc`, `curve`, `rrect`, `closed`, `ink`, the colors, and `arms()`, called at the point in the pen's order where its arms and hands are drawn. Parts the player moves: `body`, `cap` (tips), `eye0`/`eye1` (blink), `pupil0`/`pupil1` (look), `squint0`/`squint1`, `brows:<name>` and `mouth:<name>` for the feelings (`up`, `high`, `worried`, `quizzical`, `sad`; `smile`, `grin`, `wow`, `wavy`, `side`, `frown`), and `floor`. |
| `anchors` | Where each shared costume it wears sits on its body, as a scale and a shift of the place it was made for on the marker: `hat` (on its head), `neck` (a bow tie, a scarf), `chest` (a star), `hair` (a bow), `ear` (a pencil): `{ s, du, dv }`. Every costume in `wear` needs its place here (glasses sit over its eyes), and a place is never taken from the slot's character. |
| `wear`, `skins` | The shared costumes that fit its body (none when it is left out): a hat only if it has a head, a bow tie or a scarf only if it has a neck, `glasses` only if it has none of its own; and its skins (`plain` at least). |
| `costumes` | Its own costumes, from its drawings: `{ draw(k), hat, top }`, `hat: true` for a hat or anything else that sits on its head (one at a time), `top` how far above its head it reaches. |
| `outfits` | Optional: costumes worn together, by name, `[{ name, wear }]`: those of each variant picture (Stuart's `winter` and `Halloween`), and any it has for an occasion of the year (`Christmas`, `Fourth of July`) or a part it plays (`builder`, `chef`). A board or a host asks for an outfit by its name, so each character wears its own. Its presentations wear them, one each. |
| `motion` | What it is made of: `squash` and `stretch` as multiples of the kit's own, `freq` and `damp` (how fast it wobbles after a landing and how fast that dies), `jelly` (how much its top sways over its feet, 0 for a stiff body), `breath` and `breathPeriod`. Leave it empty for the marker's stiff barrel. |
| `personality` | `traits` (a line), `intros` and `outros` (its own ways of arriving and leaving; the seed picks among them), `shy` (a pose of its own it takes, shy, as it arrives and in its first quiet moment), `travel` (its ways from place to place), `quirks` (what it does on its own in a quiet moment: a pose of its own, like Stuart's `glasses`, which needs its pose in `poses`; one of its `forms`; or one of its `fidgets`). |
| `swing` | Optional: its parts that move on their own, `{ <part>: { pivot, deg, period, sides, while } }`: the `[u, v]` it turns about, how far (degrees) and how fast (seconds a sway), how often it swings over to its other side (seconds), and, for a part still until a fidget, which. Each part is drawn under that name. |
| `views` | Optional: its other views, `{ <name>: { hat, wear, middle } }`, each drawn in `draw(k)` inside `k.view(<name>, () => ({ eyes }))`, and taken by the poses that say `view: <name>` (Another view of it); for a solid, each with its `yaw`, and the drawings only a turn passes through with `between: true`. |
| `yaw` | Optional, for a character drawn from a modelled shape: the angle its standing view is seen from (its side 0, facing the viewer 90), so it turns by rotating (Another view of it, A solid turns by rotating). |
| `forms` | Optional: the names of its whole-body forms (`["sit", "curl"]`), each drawn as `form:<name>`. |
| `fidgets` | Optional: `{ <name>: { time, look } }`, a quirk that moves the parts whose `swing` names it, `time` seconds long, with `look: "around"` to look about meanwhile. |

The intros a character can choose from are `wave`, `hop`, `spin`, `bow`, `tip` (it needs a cap), `jump`, for a soft body `bounce` (it squashes, springs up, lands and bounces once more) and `jiggle` (it shakes itself awake like a jelly), for an animal that rubs against you to say hello, `rub`, and for a gymnast, `flip` and `roll`; the outros, `wave`, `bow`, `cheer`, `salute`, `tip`, `spin`, `bounce`, for one that waves its tail and walks away, `stroll`, and `flip` and `roll`, which leave the same way.

## How every character points, stands and moves, and stays in the picture

Whatever its body, the guide points at a thing only when the way is clear: the straight line from its shoulder to it crosses nothing else on the board, and, when the thing is out of its reach, nothing else lies just ahead of its finger before it (the eye follows a finger to the first thing it meets) and its finger does not stop in the air beside another thing. A thing and its own note never block each other, and across the cells of a thing's own table the hand points at the table. When the way is not clear, or the thing is out of reach and a place nearer has a clear way, it steps (one small hop) to free paper beside or under the thing, inside the shot and on the board; at most one step a sentence, never two within two seconds. Where no place serves, it only looks, its hands at rest. A thing not drawn yet never stops its hand.

Nothing it does hides the drawing. A step belongs to its sentence: it leaves once the sentence has begun, so the last picture is seen whole with the guide still beside it, and the sentence waits the moment the step takes, so the pen begins as it lands. A step never crosses what is drawn, for a body that covers a thing even in passing hides it while the camera holds still: it hops over what is low enough, goes round in two hops by free paper, or does not go. It never stands on a thing, against one, or with its feet on top of one (it would seem to stand on it, as on a shelf). Its place in each idea is the free paper from which its hand reaches the most of the idea along clear lines, so it needs to step less, and never one that makes the idea's shot so wide its words get small or its paper empty. The audit warns where it stands on a thing all the same (the `stand` rule).

It is whole in the picture whenever the camera holds still: it lands from a hop between ideas as the camera arrives, with as many hops as the camera's flight has time for and never higher than the shot it lands in has room for, and it steps only inside the shot and on the board, never higher than the shot has room for over either end of the step. The audit fails a board where it is not (the `guide` rule).

A character's `size` (1 for the marker) scales it beside the others: a character as wide as it is tall stands a little shorter (Stuart: 0.76), so it takes about the paper the marker takes and the camera does not pull back to fit it.
